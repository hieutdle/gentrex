APPENDIX 5: SUMMARY OF THE TEST

Laboratory process. {% if kind == "exome" %}Exome sequencing is performed on genomic DNA with a hybridization capture kit that targets the coding exons of nearly all human genes and selected non-coding regions.{% elif kind == "mito" %}The whole mitochondrial genome is amplified in two overlapping long-range fragments and sequenced to a median depth above 1000x, so that heteroplasmy down to 5% is detected.{% else %}The coding exons and 20 base pairs of flanking intronic sequence of the genes of the panel are captured and sequenced, together with selected non-coding variants that are known to cause disease.{% endif %} Sequencing is performed on a massively parallel sequencing platform.

Bioinformatics and quality control. Reads are aligned to the GRCh38 reference sequence. A validated pipeline calls single nucleotide variants and small indels, and read depth is used to detect copy number variants. Any variant below the quality thresholds is confirmed by Sanger sequencing. {{ hgvs_example or "" }}

Interpretation. Variants are classified with the ACMG/AMP guidelines into five classes. Benign and likely benign variants are not reported. The classification relies on population data, the literature, computational predictions and the clinical information provided.

Limitations. The test does not detect low-level mosaicism, balanced rearrangements, repeat expansions, or variants in regions of the genome that are not covered with sufficient quality. A negative result does not exclude a genetic cause of the patient's condition.
